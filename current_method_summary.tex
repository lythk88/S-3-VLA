\section{Success-Guided Safe Flow Matching with Grasp-Conditioned Compound Geometry}

The proposed method augments a flow-matching manipulation policy with a learned success critic and a discrete control-barrier-function (DCBF) quadratic program (QP). The nominal policy retains task competence, the critic supplies a task-progress direction, and the QP computes the smallest bounded correction that is compatible with predicted safety.

\subsection{Nominal flow-matching policy}

Given observation $o_k$ and language instruction $\ell$, the policy generates an action chunk $A_\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{H_\pi\times d_a}$ by integrating the learned conditional velocity field from the noise endpoint to the action endpoint:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{d}A_\tau}{\mathrm{d}\tau}
    =v_\theta\!\left(A_\tau,\tau\mid o_k,\ell\right),
    \qquad
    A_{\tau_{\mathrm{n}}}\sim p_{\mathrm{n}},
    \qquad
    A_{\tau_{\mathrm{a}}}=\Pi_\theta(o_k,\ell).
\end{equation}
Only a receding prefix is executed before the policy is queried again. Thus, perception, success guidance, and safety constraints are repeatedly updated in closed loop.

\subsection{Success-critic training and guidance}

For each training rollout $i$, the denoising trace provides policy hidden states $Z_{i,k,\tau}$, noisy action chunks $A_{i,k,\tau}$, denoising time $\tau$, and the rollout-level success label $y_i\in\{0,1\}$. The critic combines projected hidden tokens, projected action tokens, a time embedding $\psi(\tau)$, and learned positional embeddings. A transformer encoder followed by learned-query attention pooling produces a success logit and probability:
\begin{equation}
    z_\phi
    =f_\phi\!\left(Z_{i,k,\tau},A_{i,k,\tau},\psi(\tau)\right),
    \qquad
    V_\phi\!\left(Z_{i,k,\tau},A_{i,k,\tau},\tau\right)
    =\sigma(z_\phi).
\end{equation}
The data split is performed at rollout level and stratified by task and evaluation condition, preventing samples from the same rollout from appearing in both training and validation sets. The critic is optimized using class-balanced binary cross-entropy. Failure samples may be weighted more strongly near the end of a rollout through the normalized progress $\rho_{i,k}$:
\begin{equation}
    w_{i,k}=
    \begin{cases}
        w_{+}(\rho_{i,k}), & y_i=1,\\
        w_{-}(\rho_{i,k}), & y_i=0,
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    w_{-}(\rho)=w_{-,\mathrm{start}}
    +\left(w_{-,\mathrm{end}}-w_{-,\mathrm{start}}\right)\rho .
\end{equation}
The training objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{critic}}(\phi)
    =-
    \frac{1}{\sum_n w_n}
    \sum_n w_n
    \left[
        \alpha_{+}y_n\log V_{\phi,n}
        +\alpha_{-}(1-y_n)\log\!\left(1-V_{\phi,n}\right)
    \right],
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{+}$ and $\alpha_{-}$ compensate for class imbalance. Model selection uses validation probability calibration through the Brier score:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{val}}(\phi)
    =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}|}
    \sum_{n\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{val}}}
    \left(V_{\phi,n}-y_n\right)^2.
\end{equation}
At inference time, the success direction is the action-space gradient of log success probability. The critic evaluates the complete policy chunk, while the QP acts on a selectable prefix through a linear selection operator $S_q$:
\begin{equation}
    g_{\mathrm{succ}}
    =S_q\nabla_{A_\tau}
    \log V_\phi\!\left(Z_\tau,A_\tau,\tau\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Obstacle and gripper geometry}

RGB--D observations are back-projected into a world-frame point cloud. Obstacle evidence from the available camera views is fitted by selecting a primitive from a candidate family $\mathcal{P}$, such as an axis-aligned box, cylinder, or sphere:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{P}
    =\underset{P\in\mathcal{P}}{\operatorname{argmin}}
    \left[
        \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{fit}}(P;\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{obs}})
        +\lambda_{\mathrm{shape}}\mathcal{C}(P)
    \right].
\end{equation}
An optional one-sided vertical padding $p_z^{+}$ is applied to the upper obstacle extent, while the other extents remain governed by independent symbolic padding parameters. The gripper is represented by an oriented ellipsoid with center $c_k$, orientation $R_k$, and semi-axis vector $r_g$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_k
    =\left\{
        x:\left(R_k^{\top}(x-c_k)\right)^{\top}
        \operatorname{diag}(r_g^{-2})
        \left(R_k^{\top}(x-c_k)\right)\leq 1
    \right\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Grasp-conditioned carried-object compound model}

Carried-object estimation is triggered by a commanded closing motion together with a measured decrease in gripper width. With thresholds $\eta_a$ and $\eta_w$, the trigger is
\begin{equation}
    \chi_k
    =\mathbb{I}\!\left[
        u^{\mathrm{grip}}_k\geq\eta_a
        \;\land\;
        \left(w_{k-1}-w_k\right)\geq\eta_w
    \right].
\end{equation}
The method then back-projects the agent-view RGB--D image into a colored world-frame point set $\mathcal{X}_k$. Let $e_z^{-}$ denote the world-down direction. The support distance and bottom point of the gripper ellipsoid are
\begin{equation}
    s_g=\left\|r_g\odot R_k^{\top}e_z^{-}\right\|_2,
    \qquad
    b_k=c_k+s_g e_z^{-}.
\end{equation}
Candidate carried-object points are selected geometrically around this bottom point:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{crop}}_k
    =\left\{
        x\in\mathcal{X}_k:
        \left\|(I-e_z^{-}{e_z^{-}}^{\top})(x-b_k)\right\|_2\leq r_c,
        \quad
        -d_c^{-}\leq {e_z^{-}}^{\top}(x-b_k)\leq d_c^{+}
    \right\}.
\end{equation}
The dominant horizontal support plane is removed without imposing a global world-height cutoff. Points inside inflated gripper and obstacle geometry are also removed. The remaining XYZ--color points are voxelized and clustered. A cluster $C$ is ranked using proximity to the attachment region, proximity to the crop center, and point support:
\begin{equation}
    s(C)
    =d_{\mathrm{anchor}}(C)
    +\lambda_c d_{\mathrm{center}}(C)
    -\lambda_n\log\!\left(1+|C|\right),
    \qquad
    C^{\star}=\underset{C}{\operatorname{argmin}}\;s(C).
\end{equation}
After rejecting geometrically implausible clusters, trimmed coordinate quantiles define a world-axis-aligned carried-object box with half extents $r_c^{\mathrm{obj}}$:
\begin{equation}
    x^{-}=Q_{q^{-}}(C^{\star})-p_c,
    \qquad
    x^{+}=Q_{q^{+}}(C^{\star})+p_c,
    \qquad
    r_c^{\mathrm{obj}}=\frac{x^{+}-x^{-}}{2}.
\end{equation}
The fitted dimensions are retained, whereas the runtime box is attached directly to the gripper-ellipsoid bottom. It remains axis aligned and translates with the gripper:
\begin{equation}
    c^{\mathrm{obj}}_k
    =c_k+\left(s_g+r^{\mathrm{obj}}_{c,z}\right)e_z^{-},
    \qquad
    \mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{obj}}_k
    =\left\{x:\left|x-c^{\mathrm{obj}}_k\right|\preceq r_c^{\mathrm{obj}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Thus, the protected robot geometry after grasp is the compound body
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}_k
    =
    \begin{cases}
        \mathcal{E}_k, & \chi_{0:k}=0,\\
        \mathcal{E}_k\cup\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{obj}}_k, & \chi_{0:k}=1,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where activation is persistent for the remainder of the grasp phase. Following activation, the stale action suffix is discarded and the nominal policy is queried again.

\subsection{Success-guided DCBF quadratic program}

For a nominal prefix $A=(a_0,\ldots,a_{H_q-1})$, a controller-aware predictor propagates the gripper and compound geometry:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{x}_{j+1}=F_{\kappa}(\widehat{x}_j,a_j),
    \qquad
    \widehat{x}_0=x_k,
    \qquad
    j\in\{0,\ldots,H_q-1\},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ denotes the estimated action-to-motion response. Let $d(\mathcal{S},\widehat{P})$ be the signed geometric clearance between a protected shape and the obstacle. The compound safety barrier is the minimum clearance of its components:
\begin{equation}
    h_j(A)
    =
    \min\!\left{
        d(\mathcal{E}_j,\widehat{P}),
        d(\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{obj}}_j,\widehat{P})
    \right}
    -d_{\mathrm{safe}},
\end{equation}
with the carried-object term omitted before compound activation. Around the nominal chunk, each predicted barrier is linearized as
\begin{equation}
    h_j(A+S_q^{\top}\delta)
    \approx \widehat{h}_j+J_j\delta,
    \qquad
    \widehat{h}_j=h_j(A),
    \qquad
    J_j=\left.\frac{\partial h_j}{\partial\delta}\right|_{\delta=0}.
\end{equation}
The correction $\delta$ is found by balancing nominal-action preservation and ascent of the learned success value:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \delta^{\star}
    ={}&\underset{\delta}{\operatorname{argmin}}
        && \lambda_{\mathrm{dev}}\|\delta\|_2^2
        -\beta_{\mathrm{succ}}g_{\mathrm{succ}}^{\top}\delta \\
    &\operatorname{subject\ to}
        && \widehat{h}_j+J_j\delta
        \geq(1-\gamma)
        \left(\widehat{h}_{j-1}+J_{j-1}\delta\right),
        \quad j\in\{1,\ldots,H_q\},\\
    &&& \|\delta\|_2\leq r_{\mathrm{trust}},\\
    &&& a_{\min}\preceq A+S_q^{\top}\delta\preceq a_{\max},\\
    &&& \delta_{j,d}=0,
        \quad d\notin\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trans}}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $\gamma$ is the DCBF decay parameter, $r_{\mathrm{trust}}$ bounds intervention magnitude, and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{trans}}$ restricts corrections to translational action components. The measured current barrier supplies the initial DCBF value. Feasibility may first be sought over an ordered set of trust radii.

If the hard barrier constraints are infeasible, the fail-soft branch solves an auxiliary max--min linear program. Let $\underline{\delta}$ and $\overline{\delta}$ combine the correction-authority and action-bound constraints. The largest attainable common affine barrier floor is
\begin{equation}
    (\bar{\delta},z^{\star})
    =\underset{\delta,z}{\operatorname{argmax}}\;z
    \quad\operatorname{subject\ to}\quad
    \widehat{h}_j+J_j\delta\geq z,
    \qquad
    \underline{\delta}\preceq\delta\preceq\overline{\delta}.
\end{equation}
A secondary QP then finds the minimum-deviation, success-guided correction that preserves this best attainable floor:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \delta_{\mathrm{fs}}^{\star}
    ={}&\underset{\delta}{\operatorname{argmin}}
        &&\lambda_{\mathrm{dev}}\|\delta\|_2^2
        -\beta_{\mathrm{succ}}g_{\mathrm{succ}}^{\top}\delta\\
    &\operatorname{subject\ to}
        &&\widehat{h}_j+J_j\delta\geq z^{\star}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{lp}},\\
    &&&\underline{\delta}\preceq\delta\preceq\overline{\delta}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The candidate is accepted only if re-evaluation with the exact nonlinear geometry improves the minimum barrier. A nonnegative exact barrier certifies safety. If the best exact barrier remains negative, the correction is only locally least unsafe: a strict execution mode rejects the action, whereas an explicitly enabled continuation mode may execute it while recording an unsafe status.

The corrected prefix is inserted back into the full action chunk,
\begin{equation}
    A^{+}=A+S_q^{\top}\delta^{\star},
\end{equation}
and flow integration continues from $A^{+}$. Before each executed action, safety is re-evaluated with the latest observation and the QP is solved again over the current prefix; only its first corrected action is applied. After $H_{\mathrm{exec}}$ actions, the nominal policy generates a fresh chunk. This nested receding-horizon procedure allows the policy to reuse a longer semantic plan while enforcing short-horizon safety in closed loop.
